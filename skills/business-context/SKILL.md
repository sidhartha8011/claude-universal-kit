# Business Context

The facts about the business live in `references/business.md`. Claims not in that file are not facts.

## Order of work

1. Read `references/business.md` before writing any claim about the company, its work, or its results.
2. Use the offer names, pricing shape, and positioning line exactly as recorded. Don't paraphrase a service name into something that sounds better.
3. For any number, client name, or outcome: cite it from the reference. If it isn't there, write `[NEEDS: …]` and flag it in your report — do not estimate, round up, or generate a plausible figure.
4. Differentiate against the competitors listed, on the axes listed. Don't invent a rivalry or disparage a named competitor.

## Hard rules

- **No fabricated proof.** No invented clients, revenue figures, headcount, years in business, awards, or "trusted by 500+ brands".
- Don't claim capabilities outside the recorded service list. If asked to sell something the business doesn't offer, say so.
- Keep the recorded legal/company name and contact details exact — including the canonical email. If the reference flags a contact detail as unconfirmed, use `[NEEDS: …]` rather than a placeholder that could ship.
- Pricing: only state what the reference authorises stating publicly.
- Positioning is one line and it does not change per piece. Supporting angles can vary.

## Composing with other skills

- `voice-dna` — how the claim sounds.
- `audience-profile` — which persona the positioning is aimed at, and which competitor they're really comparing against.
- `content-marketing` — pipeline for multi-channel work; this skill supplies the facts each stage cites.

## Keeping it current

Editable template. Update `references/business.md` whenever a service, price, positioning line, goal, or proof point changes — stale business facts ship as public errors, which is worse than a missing one.
