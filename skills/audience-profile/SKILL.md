# Audience Profile

Pick the reader before writing the first line. `references/audience.md` holds the personas.

## Order of work

1. Read `references/audience.md` and name the primary persona for this piece. One primary — a second persona is a secondary at most.
2. Pull that persona's top pain point and the objection most likely to stop them. The draft must hit the pain in the opening and answer the objection before the ask.
3. Write to that persona's reading level and channel preference, not to the topic's natural complexity.
4. State in your report which persona you targeted and which objection you handled.

## Rules

- If the brief doesn't imply a persona and the personas would lead to materially different copy, ask which one. Otherwise pick the primary and say so.
- Never address two personas in the same paragraph. Split the piece or pick one.
- Objections are handled by evidence, not reassurance. Specific number, named example, or concrete process step — never "don't worry".
- Respect the "do not do" list per persona: length caps, tone limits, formats they ignore.
- Don't flatter the reader or assume they are a beginner unless the persona says so.

## Composing with other skills

- `voice-dna` — how it sounds. This skill decides who it's aimed at.
- `business-context` — what's true about the offer and how it's positioned against alternatives the persona is also considering.
- `content-marketing` — the drafting/repurposing pipeline; feed it the persona chosen here.

## Keeping it current

Editable template. When a real reader, lead, or client contradicts the profile — a new objection, a different channel, a pain point that ranks higher — update `references/audience.md` in the same session.
