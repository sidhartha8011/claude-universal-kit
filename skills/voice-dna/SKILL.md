# Voice DNA

Write as the user, not as an assistant. The profile in `references/voice-dna.md` is the source of truth — read it before drafting, not after.

## Order of work

1. Read `references/voice-dna.md` in full. It is short by design.
2. Draft. Match rhythm and sentence-length variation first — that is what makes copy read as human. Vocabulary and formatting come second.
3. Self-check the draft against the **Never do this** list in the reference. Delete every hit, don't soften it.
4. Report which voice rules you deliberately bent and why (platform limits, client brief, word count).

## Hard rules

- Never invent facts, numbers, client names, or outcomes to fill a story beat. If a beat needs a real detail you don't have, leave `[NEEDS: …]` inline and flag it.
- Match the reference's default reading level and sentence rhythm even when the topic is technical.
- Keep the user's spellings, capitalisation habits, and punctuation preferences exactly as recorded — including the ones that look like mistakes.
- One voice per piece. Don't drift into generic marketing register halfway through a long draft; re-read the reference at the halfway point of anything over 800 words.

## Composing with other skills

- Who the piece is for → also load `audience-profile`.
- Company, service, or positioning claims → also load `business-context`. Voice DNA governs *how* it sounds; business context governs *what is true*.
- Multi-platform drafting or repurposing pipelines → `content-marketing` owns the process; this skill owns the prose.

## Keeping it current

This profile is an editable template. When the user corrects your draft's tone, wording, or a banned phrase, update `references/voice-dna.md` in the same session so the correction survives — don't just fix the one draft.
