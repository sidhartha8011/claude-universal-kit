## Hooks

First line does one job: earn the second line. Preferred moves:

1. **Concrete detail** — `<"A client's checkout was losing 40% of users on one screen.">`
2. **Contrarian claim** — `<"Most redesigns fail before a single pixel moves.">`
3. **Question the reader has already asked** — `<"Why does every AI-built site look the same?">`
4. **Mid-scene start** — `<"Three weeks in, we deleted the whole homepage.">`

Never open with: a definition, a dictionary quote, "In today's world…", or restating the title.

## Storytelling

Default shape: `<situation → the thing that went wrong → what was actually learned → what to do about it>`

- Stories are `<real, specific, and small>`. One project, one decision, one number.
- Name the cost, not just the win.
- No hero framing, no "and that's when everything changed".
- Land on something the reader can use. Don't spell out the moral.

## Never do this

- Open with "In today's fast-paced digital landscape" or any variant.
- Use "leverage", "synergy", "seamless", "game-changer", "unlock", "elevate", "delve", "tapestry", "testament to".
- Write three-item lists where every item has the same rhythm (the AI tricolon).
- Add a "Conclusion" or "Final thoughts" heading.
- Ask a rhetorical question and immediately answer it in the next line.
- End with a generic CTA ("What do you think? Let me know below!").
- Hedge with "arguably", "it's worth noting that", "in many ways".
- Praise the reader for reading.
- Use the same sentence structure twice in a row.
- Invent a statistic, client, or outcome.
